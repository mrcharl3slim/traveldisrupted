How to test it

Ten things to try, with what to expect. What this instance is running on lives on /developer; the presenter's run-book on /script.

Test 1 · Book a trip

  1. Open / and type one way zurich to milan on 18 september with a hotel, cheapest
  2. Answer whatever it asks. Every question says why it is being asked — if one does not change the answer, that is a bug.
  3. Check the confirmation card. Dates are spelled out in full on purpose. Say no and correct something, then say yes.
  4. Pick a flight or a train, pick a room, confirm.
Expect: trains and flights in one list, ranked by what you asked for. Under cheapest the S$108 train ranks with the fares on price alone; under fastest a 55-minute flight wins. Both are correct.
Not a bug: answering a question with something it cannot read leaves the question open and says so. That is deliberate — a silently dropped answer is worse.

Test 2 · Break one leg

Click the trip in the panel on the right — the itinerary opens in the centre, above the conversation — click its heading to fold it away. Every flight has three controls: a duration, Late, and Cancel.

  1. Try 30m late first. Then the same leg at 6h or 12h.
  2. Then Cancel a leg and compare.
  3. Pick a recovery plan. It opens line by line, one box each — untick the hotel email, press Take this plan, and check it says declined rather than quietly dropping it. Then try unticking the purchase: the trip should stay broken and the watch keep counting.
Expect: a small delay costs nothing — the connection absorbs it. Past the buffer, the onward leg breaks and the room goes at risk. A cancellation strands you where you started; a delay lands you late. Those are different worlds and the plans differ.
Also expect: the delayed leg keeps its place, marked delayed, with the original time kept beside it as reference — late is not gone. And pressing Take this plan twice (a double-click, a stale tab) must answer already handled the second time, not quietly rewrite the trip again.
Not a bug: the replacement flight is marked not paid. It is an intention, not a purchase, and it is not allowed to look like one.

Test 3 · Add a meeting

In the same box on /, type something nobody sold you:

meeting with the Milan team on 19 September at 10am at their office

Expect: it chases exactly three things — who, when, where — and guesses none of them. Once saved it is checked against your flights: a meeting you cannot reach is reported, and it is counted, never priced. There is no euro figure on a missed meeting because we would have to invent one.
Worth trying: give it a location it cannot place (at the Ritz Carlton). It should ask which city, because the city is the clock — not decoration.

Test 4 · Call the whole trip off

  1. Press Cancel the whole trip under the itinerary.
  2. Read the price before you confirm. Then confirm.
Expect: one errand per booking, sorted by who can do it — emailed for you, one tap, or a phone call. What comes back plus what is gone equals what you paid. Every row then reads as cancelled and names its errand.
Not a bug: the number moves depending on when you press it. A free-cancellation window open this morning is shut tonight, and the page says which moment it was worked out at.

Test 5 · Let it act on its own

  1. Book a trip, then add a meeting in Milan that evening (Test 3).
  2. Under the itinerary, set act without asking up to 300 and save.
  3. Cancel the onward leg.
Expect: the plan is taken before the page finishes loading — I have taken EC 11:33 — within your S$300 limit — the hotel is emailed, the itinerary is rewritten, and the purchase link is left for you. The reason reads like a sentence: keeps the meeting; costs S$108 more than doing nothing, which would miss it. Set the cap to 50 and repeat: it waits, and says by how much it is over.
Then say it once: in the User profile panel on /, set home singapore, sort by cheapest, act up to 300. Type one way to zurich on 18 september, no hotel — it should go straight to the confirmation card with From where: Singapore · from your profile, and the next trip you book already carries the S$300 rule. Then type zurich to milan — Zurich must win over the profile.
Not a bug: nothing was bought. Pre-authorised means the agent decided; the purchase is still yours. Also: the watch asks the live status feed about your flights every ten minutes on its own, but only within a week of departure — so a trip three weeks out will not be found late by itself. Use the buttons.

Test 6 · Let somebody else in

  1. With a trip open on /, find Who else sees this under the itinerary. Share it with a name as host; copy the link it shows you.
  2. Open that link in a private window. You are now the host.
  3. Do the same as assistant and as finance.
Expect: the host sees every leg with its times and no prices anywhere — not on the rows, not in the alerts, not after a delay. The host has no buttons. The assistant has all of them and their name appears in the record when they take a plan. Finance can change the spending cap and gets 403 if they try to cancel a leg. A link you were not given shows an empty list, not somebody else's trips.
Not a bug, and worth knowing: there is no login. The link is the identity — anyone holding it is that person. A fresh browser quietly mints its own identity on first visit, so two strangers can never see each other's trips. Losing a link means remove and share again; it cannot be looked up. "Forget it" in the User profile panel drops the token from this browser.

Test 7 · Where the trip is thin

Right after booking (Test 1), look at the itinerary — before anything is broken at all.

Expect: a where this trip is thin section, flagged on day one: the two flights are separate purchases with the minutes to spare named — nobody owes you that connection — and each leg carries the smallest delay that starts costing money. The connection it warns about is exactly the one Test 2 breaks. Derived from booked times and the engine's own stress test; no probabilities, no weather feed.
Not a bug: a train two days after landing is not flagged. An overnight decouples two bookings — burying the one flag that matters under true-but-useless ones would be worse than silence.

Test 8 · The tiers, then the kill switch

  1. In the trip's rules, set act ≤ 300 and hold ≤ 500. Save, then Cancel a leg whose best plan costs between the two.
  2. Press Resume automation, then press Pause automation (the kill switch) and cancel again. It applies to this trip only and takes effect at once — no Save.
Expect (middle tier): the agent still decides — the plan is taken and held — but zero messages leave: the hotel email waits for you and the pending purchase is the hold itself. Below S$300 it acts and sends; above S$500 it only asks.
Expect (kill switch): the same cancellation waits entirely. Every line reads needs approval with one fixed reason, nothing is sent anywhere — the email included — and deadline alerts still flow, because a switch that silences your own warnings is a worse product.
Not a bug: the plans stay visible while disarmed. Read-only means read — the switch stops the kitchen, it does not hide the menu. And a profile save must not quietly re-arm a remembered switch.

Test 9 · The paper trail

  1. After any of the above, open Paper trail under the itinerary.
  2. Export it as plain text from the same panel and read it top to bottom.
Expect: one entry per decision — extracted, assessed, judged, performed, alerted — each with a timestamp, who or what acted, the fare rule it cites, and the authorization it acted under (within your S$300 limit, declined by you, kill switch). A declined line is in there as declined, not missing.
Not a bug: the host from Test 6 cannot open it. The trail prices decisions, and the host's whole deal is seeing every time and not one price.

Test 10 · It knows where you are

  1. With a trip booked (Test 1), ask it to file a meeting in a city the itinerary never visits on a day you are abroad — meeting with the board on the 19th at 10am in Singapore.
  2. Then file one in the right city but under two hours after your flight lands, or 90 minutes after another meeting.
  3. Then just ask: when is my meeting with the client?
Expect: the wrong-city meeting is refused — not saved — and the reply names where the itinerary actually puts you that day. The tight one gets a warning and a Save it anyway? chip: yes saves, no doesn't. The question is answered from what is stored, with the time in the meeting's own timezone — and creates nothing.
Not a bug: a trip with no return leg leaves you presumed at the last destination — said as "open-ended", so a late meeting there is allowed. And a place the engine does not recognise never causes a refusal: no opinion beats an invented one.
