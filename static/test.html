What this thing is

Detecting that a flight is late is commodity. Downstream does the part after: what else in the trip breaks, what it costs, and what to do instead — across airlines, railways and hotels that do not talk to each other.

You build a real itinerary from live inventory, then break it and watch the consequences fall out of the same data. Nothing about the outcome is scripted.

Nothing is ever bought. Every provider account here is a test account that cannot take money. If you find a way to spend a real euro, that is the most important bug you will file all day.

Test 1 · Book a trip

  1. Open / and type one way zurich to milan on 18 september with a hotel, cheapest
  2. Answer whatever it asks. Every question says why it is being asked — if one does not change the answer, that is a bug.
  3. Check the confirmation card. Dates are spelled out in full on purpose. Say no and correct something, then say yes.
  4. Pick a flight or a train, pick a room, confirm.
Expect: trains and flights in one list, ranked by what you asked for. Under cheapest the €72 train sorts above every fare; under fastest a 55-minute flight wins. Both are correct.
Not a bug: answering a question with something it cannot read leaves the question open and says so. That is deliberate — a silently dropped answer is worse.

Test 2 · Break one leg

Open the itinerary in the right-hand panel (or the trip section on /book). Every flight has three controls: a duration, Late, and Cancel.

  1. Try 30m late first. Then the same leg at 6h or 12h.
  2. Then Cancel a leg and compare.
  3. Pick a recovery plan and press Take this plan.
Expect: a small delay costs nothing — the connection absorbs it. Past the buffer, the onward leg breaks and the room goes at risk. A cancellation strands you where you started; a delay lands you late. Those are different worlds and the plans differ.
Not a bug: the replacement flight is marked not paid. It is an intention, not a purchase, and it is not allowed to look like one.

Test 3 · Add a meeting

In the same box on /, type something nobody sold you:

meeting with the Milan team on 19 September at 10am at their office

Expect: it chases exactly three things — who, when, where — and guesses none of them. Once saved it is checked against your flights: a meeting you cannot reach is reported, and it is counted, never priced. There is no euro figure on a missed meeting because we would have to invent one.
Worth trying: give it a location it cannot place (at the Ritz Carlton). It should ask which city, because the city is the clock — not decoration.

Test 4 · Call the whole trip off

  1. Press Cancel the whole trip under the itinerary.
  2. Read the price before you confirm. Then confirm.
Expect: one errand per booking, sorted by who can do it — emailed for you, one tap, or a phone call. What comes back plus what is gone equals what you paid. Every row then reads as cancelled and names its errand.
Not a bug: the number moves depending on when you press it. A free-cancellation window open this morning is shut tonight, and the page says which moment it was worked out at.

Known limits — please do not file these

What you will seeWhy
“No inventory recorded for that route” In replay only recorded routes exist. See the box at the top for which. On live any route works.
Trains only between Zurich and Milan The only rail API we can reach is the Swiss timetable. It does not know about Madrid.
A train fare is marked estimated SBB publishes no free fare API. The number is ours and says so everywhere it appears — including in totals.
Trips vanish after a while Storage says memory in the box above. A free instance sleeps and takes them with it.
A replacement leg says not paid Deliberate. Nothing here can take money, and a plan must not look like a purchase.
“You will have to call” That provider has no consumer API. Saying so is the feature — the alternative is claiming we did something we cannot.

Filing a bug

Four things, and it is almost always enough:

  • The page and what you typed — the exact sentence, not a summary of it.
  • What you expected and what you got. A number being wrong is more useful than a number being odd — tell us which one you can check by hand.
  • The trip id, from the address bar on /book (?trip=…).
  • The line from the box at the top of this page, copied. It says which providers answered and which were replayed, and that changes the answer.
A screenshot of the whole page beats a crop. Half the interesting failures are two numbers that disagree with each other.
