The demo, as a script

Seven acts, about six minutes. Every line to type is a box; every line to say is in italics.

Before you start

reading /health…

Open / a few minutes early — a free instance takes ~30s to wake. If the User profile panel or an old trip is left over from a previous run, press forget it and clear the profile fields first: the script assumes a blank slate. Keep /demo in a second tab for the closer.

Short on time, or the room is remote? /story runs these same chapters through the engine in one press and shows the computed figures — this script is the same arc performed by hand.

The one sentence to hold the room with before touching anything:

Detection is commodity — every travel app can tell you your flight is late. This is the part after: what else in the trip breaks, what that costs, and an agent that acts on it — within rules you set, never with your money.

The script

0:00

Act 1 — Say it once

In the User profile panel: home singapore, sort by cheapest, act up to 300. Save.

Everything I tell it here is said once, applied to every trip, and shown on screen whenever it is used. The S$300 is the interesting one — it is what I pre-authorise the agent to decide without waking me.
0:40

Act 2 — A sentence, not a form

one way to zurich on 18 september, then milan, with a hotel …or tap the starter chip. Answer what it asks; every question shows why it is being asked.
It asks only what changes the answer — and look at the card: From where: Singapore · from your profile. It filled the blank, said so, and one word would override it.

At the options: point at the trains sitting above the flights, then pick the morning flight to Milan and a room. Confirm.

One list, both modes — under cheapest the S$108 train beats every fare, and the fare marked estimated is marked because no API sells Swiss rail tickets. We do not invent numbers quietly.
1:50

Act 3 — The reason the trip exists

meeting with the client on 18 september at 10pm in milan Attach it to the trip when asked, and save.
A meeting has no fare, so most engines treat it as free to miss. Here it becomes a booking with a commitment flag — counted, never priced — and it is about to decide a ranking.
2:30

Act 4 — Break it, twice

Open the trip in the panel. On the Milan leg: choose 30m, press Late.

Thirty minutes late: nothing is at risk. The connection absorbs it, and it says so instead of crying wolf — that restraint is why the alerts that do fire mean something.

Now press Cancel on the same leg.

Watch what came back before I touched anything: “I have taken EC 11:33 — within your S$300 limit.” It searched airlines and the railway, ranked every option against doing nothing, kept the meeting — the S$108 train beat cheaper inaction because inaction misses the client — emailed the hotel, rewrote the itinerary, and left me one purchase link. Nothing was bought. That is the difference between an alert and an agent.
Read the Recommended because… sentence aloud — it is computed, not written by a model. Then point at the plan table: Do nothing is scored by the same arithmetic and loses on the merits.
4:00

Act 5 — You stay in charge, per line

Click a different plan row. It opens line by line with a box each — untick the hotel email, press Take this plan.

Approve all, or line by line. The unticked line is recorded as declined, not forgotten — if the room goes unheld, the history says whose call that was.
4:40

Act 6 — Other people, by what their job needs

Under the itinerary: Who else sees this → share as host → open the link in a private window, side by side.

The person meeting them in Milan sees every leg, every time — and not one price, on any screen, including the disruption we just caused. An assistant would get the buttons too; finance can raise the S$300 and cannot spend a cent. No accounts — the link is the key, shown once, revocable there.
5:20

Act 7 — The numbers are checkable

Switch to the /demo tab: the fixed October trip, S$423.

Nothing on any of these pages is a typed number. This scripted trip asserts S$423 in the tests, and python cli.py prints the same figures in a terminal with no server at all — anyone leaning over a laptop can check the claim in ten seconds. And /health tells you, right now, which providers answered live and which were replayed. The whole product is that honesty, applied to a travel disruption.

If it goes sideways

What happenedDo this, say this
“No inventory recorded for that route” You are in replay and typed an unrecorded route. Stay on Singapore → Zurich → Milan, 18 September. “Replay means the demo cannot be killed by someone else's outage — the same honesty badge you saw on /health.”
degraded appears in a banner A live provider timed out and a recording answered. Say so — “that is the app refusing to hide a fallback” — it is a feature, mid-demo.
The agent did not auto-take The best plan is over S$300 or the trip missed the cap. Show the “exceeds your limit” line — the refusal IS the permission engine working — and press Take this plan yourself.
Nothing breaks when you press Late Correct: the delay fits the slack. Use a bigger delay or Cancel, and keep the point — restraint is the feature.
The instance was asleep First load takes ~30s. This is why Act 0 says open it early.
